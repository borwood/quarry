## C. Second review of the payload

### Structural suggestions

th-uacu already holds the frame (selection, hierarchy, dedup) and a candidate
mechanism — lexicon-overlap ranking of shelf entries. From this dispatch I can add
evidence and four concrete shapes:

- **The lexicon-overlap candidate works on this case.** Overlap with it-hjed's text
  and acceptance ("find", "atom_line", "lexicon", "predicate", "word") selects
  precisely dc-nnf5, cl-89b5, cl-3qwu, cl-syj7, and the it-nuw5/sc2u/b5tq family —
  the exact set the work used — and excludes fire-routing, kind-shaped-wake, and the
  report shelf. The dc-grrb tension ("bodies, not titles, so agents build from bones
  without a round-trip") resolves as two tiers: full bodies for the lexicon-matched
  few, one-line atoms for the rest, plus a count-and-reach-them line so nothing is
  silently hidden. The atom_line renderer already exists for exactly this.
- **A MAP section, derived from the item's own edges** (file: about-targets with
  blob stamps, plus any depends-on/part-of). Zero-heuristic, always earned: it is
  the item's own frontmatter. Bundled work (th-zzqv) will want this section anyway.
- **A YOUR-WRITES section**: the two or three graph acts expected of the agent at
  landing (mint kind=vein claim sourced file:..., supports toward what it stands on,
  thread-file under C3), each with its legal edge shapes. The brief currently primes
  the code work richly and leaves the graph work to archaeology — yet the graph work
  is the part harvest judges mechanically.
- **Hierarchy**: THE WORK, then MAP, then WRITE-SET + RETURN SPEC + PROTOCOL, then
  YOUR-WRITES, then the ranked shelf, then counts of the omitted. My brief put 100
  lines of shelf between the task and the contract; the contract is what I re-read
  three times during the work, scrolling past fire-routing each time.
- **Dedup rule**: first appearance renders full, every later one renders atom_ref.
  Would have removed both dc-nnf5 duplicates and shortened three shelf bodies.
- **Keep**: the derived-not-carried doctrine line at the top ("if this brief reads
  wrong, the graph is wrong — fix the graph, re-render"), the dc-dsdm one-liners,
  PROTOCOL as-is, RETURN's reflections/stop-report paragraphs as-is. These are the
  parts that visibly shaped behavior per byte spent.

### Net, against the principle

The payload's core (THE WORK, RETURN, PROTOCOL, WRITE-SET) passes the good-reason
test line by line — those sections are why a cold agent built the right thing in one
pass. The shelf fails it: by-area selection means its reason-to-exist is "the area
accumulated this," which is a reason about the graph's history, not about this
agent's next hour. And the payload's real gaps were not missing context but missing
*instruction at its own choke points* — the item's recorded file target, the legal
shapes for the writes it demands, the species its own rulings expect. Everything I
needed and lacked was already in the graph, one selection policy away — which is the
most encouraging possible failure mode: nothing needs inventing; th-uacu needs
settling.
