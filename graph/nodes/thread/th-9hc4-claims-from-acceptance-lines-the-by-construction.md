The finding (2026-08-19, defect sweep on it-kkde): the intent→vein pipeline is discipline checked after the fact, at every joint. Acceptance lines name capabilities (`name`), vein claim titles carry the names that exist, and every core surface presents the join as derivation — q query intent-delta, the shaping advertisement, the area brief's scoped delta line, the promise that landing acceptance registers a vein. But nothing constructs the match: q claim's title is free text with no feedback against open intents; --about areas are freely chosen at mint while the intending item's areas were chosen at filing; harvest lists acceptance lines as the RETURN spec and counts badge mints without ever joining their names; the assay office ratifies vein mints regardless of match. A typo'd name and a right-name-wrong-area produce the same failure signature — unlanded plus unintended, discovered later, if ever.

The user's ruling (2026-08-19): there has to be a by-construction path — preferred, offered — for claims from acceptance lines. Relying on discipline where construction is possible is a violation; discipline must never be load-bearing for what is presented as a deterministic process with core engine surfaces.

The design question: what is the constructed path from an acceptance name to its vein? Candidate ends, to be settled here:
- mint-side: a claim path that derives from the item — e.g. q claim --lands <item> <name> pulling the name, register vocabulary, and shared area from the acceptance line itself, leaving only the "what it provides" prose to author. The free-text path stays for genuinely emergent veins; the derived path is the preferred, offered default.
- harvest-side: homework prints the exact mint command per still-unlanded acceptance name of the harvested item — delivery at the moment of use, never memorized.
- mint feedback: a free-text q claim whose backticked name matches (or near-misses) an open intent says so inline.

it-kkde (disjoint-area joins) holds its narrow fix pending this thread: with a constructed path, the disjoint join mostly cannot arise, and the filed-apart report may be the only piece of the sketch that survives — as the detector for veins minted outside the constructed path.
